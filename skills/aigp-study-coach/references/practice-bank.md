# Original AIGP diagnostic: 26 questions

Unofficial learning questions, two per competency. They are not actual exam items or a psychometrically validated predictor. In coaching mode hide the answer section until the learner responds. The coverage CSV supplies additional objectives for new scenarios.

## Answers and reasoning

Q01 A. Changed deployment conditions can invalidate historical evidence. B invents a universal legal category; C denies testability; D confuses performance with impact.

Q02 C. Harm can persist despite accurate optimization. A and B ignore broader impacts; D asserts a legal conclusion without applicability analysis.

Q03 B. The organization needs actionable authority during an incident. Model size, logos and presentation volume do not resolve the gap.

Q04 A and C. Role-specific competence and relevant challenge improve decisions. Outsourcing all accountability, universal board escalation and attendance-only measures are inadequate.

Q05 C. Integrating missing lifecycle controls is proportionate. Existing controls may remain valuable; supplier continuity does not imply unchanged behavior; blanket approval ignores evidence.

Q06 B. Training is one processing purpose among several. The other options overgeneralize the promise or remove customer accountability.

Q07 B. Rights and purpose analysis precede assumptions that the data is usable. Volume and performance cannot cure unlawful or inappropriate processing.

Q08 C. Retained linkage undermines an unsupported anonymity claim. Encryption is a safeguard, not automatic exemption; publishing the key increases exposure.

Q09 D. Contractual allocation cannot guarantee absence of third-party rights. A-C are appropriate considerations.

Q10 B. Proxies and outcomes still matter. Attribute omission and aggregate accuracy do not establish absence of discrimination; applicants do not inherit the enterprise's duties.

Q11 B. Legal classification depends on facts and scope. A, C and D substitute irrelevant shortcuts.

Q12 C. Currency and transitions require verification, and exam coverage has a separate baseline. Familiarity, blanket delay and omission are not evidence.

Q13 B. It distinguishes organizational management from voluntary risk guidance. The other answers confuse credential, guidance and assurance objects.

Q14 C. Changed context can require revisiting all functions. The other choices impose a false sequence, universal checklist or unjustified omission.

Q15 B. Consequence and authority should drive design. Novelty, late review and prompt-only safeguards do not establish adequate control.

Q16 C. Early and repeated analysis can alter design and respond to change. The alternatives delay useful analysis or exclude purchased systems.

Q17 B. Optimization against test data weakens independent evidence. It does not imply all testing is unnecessary or all good results fraudulent.

Q18 B and D. Provenance, population relevance and transparent methods strengthen evidence. Leakage, selective reporting and unverified transfer of claims weaken it.

Q19 C. Documentation supports governance but does not prove operational readiness. Brand is not a control, and an evidence gap is not a universal ban.

Q20 B. Protect affected people while investigating the changed conditions. Waiting, unassessed retraining and destruction of evidence are weaker responses.

Q21 C. Hosting changes control ownership and operational duties. It does not remove data rights or determine a legal category.

Q22 A. Retrieval must respect the user's authorization. Bigger models and branding do not prove access boundaries; RAG can still produce errors.

Q23 B. Evaluate actual scope and customer-context evidence. Certificates neither cover everything nor justify categorical rejection.

Q24 A. Evaluation limits and silent changes impair assurance. They warrant investigation and negotiation, not invented universal legality conclusions.

Q25 B. Purpose change can create new harms and obligations without a model change. The other actions fail to address changed impact.

Q26 A, B and D. Exit covers continuity, authority and data/artifact handling. Indefinite access and unsupported deletion claims undermine control.
